
Original desk procedure:
Meridian Receivables | cash posting desk practice | effective 1 November 2026.
First book the bank receipt to the invoice. The invoice's gross includes goods, freight and tax. Reduce it only by a POSTED credit on the same invoice. Pending claims and draft credits have not reduced the account.
On a TERMS remittance a 2% prompt-payment discount applies to merchandise less posted merchandise credits only. Exclude freight and tax. Round half up to cents. The eligible discount cannot exceed the actual remaining shortage. Use the bank value date, never the ERP import date. After the deadline, allow no more than two Monday-Friday business days of grace, and only when the customer's payment instruction was transmitted by the deadline and Bank Operations CONFIRMED a receiving-bank interruption for this transfer. A payer-side incident or an unconfirmed notice does not qualify.
On a DAMAGE remittance transfer the smaller of the shortage, the customer's deduction, and the carrier's logged reserve to Claims, even while liability is under investigation. Require a carrier case tied to the invoiced shipment and an exception on its signed delivery proof. A customer debit note is not a posted credit. If these conditions fail leave the amount collectible. Never convert any residual on a DAMAGE remittance into a write-off.
For other residual shortages after authorized treatment, clear a small balance only if BOTH conditions hold: residual <= SGD 8.00 and residual <= 0.50% of gross invoice less actual posted credits, measured immediately before booking cash. Equal limits qualify. No partial write-off when either limit is exceeded. No write-off on a DAMAGE remittance, even when the adjustment is genuine. Separate discounts, claim transfers, small-balance write-offs, and collectible balances. Do not silently forgive a balance. Follow-up: CLAIMS if any amount transferred to claims; otherwise COLLECTIONS if any balance is collectible; otherwise CLOSED. Always report the remaining collectible amount even when Claims owns the follow-up.
